# The mascot

Drivel's mascot is a striped hyena with somewhere north of thirty heads, every
one of them hungry and every one of them drooling.

This file is the back-story and the drawing brief. Like
[marketing.md](marketing.md) it is a *source*, not a specification of the
product — but unlike marketing copy, the lore is allowed to be lore. The one
rule it inherits: nothing here may imply a capability that doesn't exist. The
beast's hunger is real. Most of its meals are not, and the story says so.

## First, a correction

For its first year the field guide called it a *spotted* hyena. The field guide
was wrong, and the error was the usual one: spots are what you draw when you
picture a hyena, not what you find when you look at this one.

Spotted hyenas run in clans, hunt in a group, and take their dinner fresh and
struggling. This animal has no clan. It hunts nothing. It eats what is in front
of it — carcass, bone, insect, fallen fruit, whatever the desert left out — and
it hauls the surplus home to a den rather than finishing it where it fell.

That is a **striped hyena**, and it always was. Opportunistic omnivore, solitary,
nocturnal, built to crack open the things other scavengers give up on, and a
hoarder by temperament. The stripes are not a redesign. They are an erratum.

## How it got the heads

The animal worked an unusual midden: the scree downhill of a data centre, where
storage goes when it dies. Tape robots with their spools still loaded. A SAN with
its controller cut off. Three geological layers of WebDAV. Nothing there is
fresh, and most of it is inedible in the ordinary sense — the meat is sealed
inside formats built specifically so that no outside animal could get at it.

Which is precisely a hyena's line of work. It has the jaws for sealed things.

The first carcass it cracked taught it the thing that made it what it is: **the
food was not the bytes. The food was the protocol.** Bytes go down easily.
A protocol cannot be swallowed at all — it can only be *spoken*. So the animal
did the only thing available to it, and grew a mouth that spoke that one.

Then it did it again. And the second time was cheaper, because only the head was
new.

A head is not a trophy and not a mutation. **A head is what a backend costs: one
mouth that speaks that dialect.** Every provider anyone ever teaches Drivel puts
another one on the pile, which is why the count is not decorative and not fixed.
The head count is a changelog.

## One stomach

All those necks converge.

Everything above the collarbone is specific to the thing it eats — the Drive head
knows about folder IDs and change cursors and nothing else in the world. Below
the collarbone there is one gullet, one stomach, and **the stomach has never
learned a single protocol, on purpose.** It handles bites. It does not know, and
must never learn, which head sent one down.

That is the whole trick of the animal. It can grow head thirty-four in an
afternoon without any of the other thirty-three noticing, and nothing it has ever
eaten can poison what comes after.

It is also why the heads do not grab. A keeper hands each one its food. Left to
themselves they would all lunge at the same carcass, and a beast that decides for
itself what it is allowed to eat is not a pet — it is an incident.

## Why they drool

Because heads are grown by appetite, they arrive hungry and they stay that way.
The drool is not decoration and it is not a gross-out gag: **the drool is the
backlog.** Every pending upload is sitting on somebody's tongue.

The corollary is the useful half. A head that stops drooling has stopped wanting
its backend, and a provider nobody is hungry for is a provider nobody maintains.
Dry mouth is how the herd culls itself.

One thing the drool is emphatically **not** aimed at: your files. The animal is
ravenous toward *providers* and careful to the point of neurosis with *carcasses*.
What it carries home, it keeps. It has never lost a bone it brought back to the
den, and the den is the entire point of the den.

## The census

Thirty-odd mouths; one of them has ever eaten a real meal. The rest are honest
about it, which is the joke and also the roadmap.

- **The Drive head** — fed daily, fed properly, visibly the fattest. The only one
  with table manners, because it is the only one that has had to learn any.
- **The hoarder's head** — recognises a bone it has already cracked and refuses to
  crack it twice. Eats leftovers exclusively. Has not been fed yet.
- **The dark head** — does not bite the carcass. Bites *through another head*,
  in the dark, so that nothing watching can tell what the meal was. Unsettling to
  the others. Not yet fed.
- **The scattered head** — eats in blocks and keeps the meal spread across a
  hundred dens at once. Not yet fed, and everyone is a little relieved.
- **The strays** — heads that are not attached to the body at all. They walk up,
  the keeper hands them something, they hand back the bones. The body never lets
  them past the collarbone. This is a compliment to the strays.

## House rules

The animal has been at this long enough to have learned some things the hard way.

- **It marks its own bites.** Once, two heads got a rhythm going: one swallowed
  what the other had just brought up, forever, and the beast nearly starved with
  its mouth full. Now every bite carries a mark, and no head eats a mark it
  recognises as its own.
- **Two heads on one carcass do not fight.** They split it, and both halves get
  a label saying who won and who did not. Nobody's dinner is thrown away to end
  an argument.
- **It bluffs the world and never its own stomach.** A raised mane makes it look
  twice its size; a carcass it has claimed but not yet fetched looks whole from
  across the valley and weighs nothing at all. That trick is for outsiders. A
  head that tries to swallow the bluff gets stopped at the throat, every time.
- **It will not eat a herd it cannot count.** If dinner suddenly appears to be
  four thousand animals, the beast concludes it is standing in the wrong valley
  rather than that it got lucky, and eats none of them.
- **Nothing is gone the moment it is swallowed.** There is a crop before the
  stomach, and things sit in it for about thirty days. Permanent digestion is a
  thing you have to ask the animal for.

## Notes for whoever draws it

- **Striped, not spotted.** Buff-grey coat, black vertical bars on the legs and
  flanks, big rounded ears, black throat patch. Erectile dorsal mane — up.
- **Head count reads as "too many to count," not as thirty-one.** Nobody should
  finish counting. A dense readable cluster beats an accurate one; keep the
  silhouette legible at favicon size, which probably means a crowded ruff of
  heads on one heavy body rather than thirty full necks.
- **Every head drools. No exceptions** — that is the joke's load-bearing beam.
  Vary the *hunger*: some heads mid-lunge, some tracking something offscreen, a
  couple asleep and drooling anyway.
- **One head is obviously better fed** than the rest and should look faintly
  smug. That is the Drive head.
- **Cartoon, not horror.** Bright, dumb, delighted. It is a good animal having
  the best day of its life. If it reads as a threat to the viewer's files, the
  drawing has said the opposite of what the program does.
- **The den is optional but excellent background**: a tidy, almost fussy stash of
  bones, all of them intact, none of them chewed.

## The files

`contrib/` holds the drawing as delivered, and everything derived from it.

**`Drivel_Logo_1.1-Official.png` is the master** — 7680×4189, fully opaque, the
animal on its own pale ground. Version 1.1 is the redraw that fixed the
erratum this file opens with: the animal is **striped** in it, where 1.0 was
spotted.

The 1.0 set — `Drivel_Logo_1.0.jpg` at 1408×768 and its two companions — was
removed once 1.1 replaced it. It is in the history if it is ever wanted, which
is where the `-verify` check below points when it finds the files gone.

Everything derived from the master is now made by **`contrib/gen-logo-assets.go`**,
which is one command and no judgement:

```sh
go install github.com/TheZoraiz/ascii-image-converter@latest   # once
go run contrib/gen-logo-assets.go
```

It writes the portrait crop (`-Cropped_1.png`), the braille rendition of that
crop (`-Cropped_1.txt`), that rendition as a two-colour image 7680px wide
(`-Braille.png`, which the website mounts as its page backdrop), and
`contrib/scaled/` — the master fitted to seven 16:9 sizes from 4K UHD down to
320×180.

Three things about it are worth knowing.

**Its parameters were recovered, not chosen.** Nothing about how the 1.0 files
were made had been written down, so the crop was found by matching the crop
against the master (a plain 1:1 cut at RMS 0.61/255, which is what proves it
was never scaled), and the braille settings by sweeping until the output
matched the committed file *byte for byte* — `-b -W 70 --threshold 99`, where
the tool's own default is 128. `go run contrib/gen-logo-assets.go -verify`
re-runs both checks against the 1.0 artefacts; run it after touching any of
those constants. It needs the 1.0 artwork, which is no longer in the tree, so it
is a flag rather than a test and it now reports that it cannot run unless those
files are checked out of the history first. The constants it guards have not
changed since it last passed.

**The crop travels as proportions**, which is the only way "the same section"
survives the master arriving at 7680×4189 instead of 1408×768.

**The scaled copies are letterboxed, not cropped.** Both masters are 1.833:1
and none of the requested sizes is, so the artwork is fitted to the width and
the leftover height padded in the ground colour sampled from the master's own
border — seamless, because the drawing does not reach its own edges. Cropping
to fill would take 3% off each side, and the outermost heads live there.

**It does not shell out for the braille by accident.** Reimplementing that step
would be about thirty lines, and the wrong thirty: the committed art is the
reference for what this art looks like, and matching it means matching that
program's choices rather than making new ones and calling them the same.

## What the website shows

Nothing under `site/` is hand-made any more. The hero image and the navbar mark
were both committed binaries with no record of how they had been produced; both
are now generated, and `site/` holds no artwork masters at all.

`gen-logo-assets.go` writes the **cut-out** — the animal with its paper removed
and a white sticker edge rebuilt at an even weight. One file serves both themes:
on a light page the edge is invisible and the animal is simply cut out, on a
dark one it reads as the sticker the drawing is styled as. The site *mounts*
it as its hero image rather than keeping a copy, so there is one file.

`gen-site-images.go` then cuts the icons out of that: the tab icons, the
`favicon.ico` a browser asks for unprompted, the home-screen icons, the navbar
mark, and the 1200×630 `og:image` card.

### Removing the background

This file used to warn that **removing the background is not a brightness
threshold**, and that every part of the joke breaks if it is treated as one.
That warning was right, and writing the cut-out proved it twice.

What works is **connectivity**: only pixels reachable from the border of the
frame are removed. The teeth are near-white and the drool is paler than the
paper, but both are sealed inside ink outlines, so no fill starting outside can
reach them. The tolerance is the middle of a measured plateau rather than a
taste — the fill takes 32.3% of the frame anywhere from 12 to 45, 36.7% from 60
to 120, and starts eating the drawing at 150. The step between those plateaus is
the soft ground shadow under the feet, which has to go: this is a sticker, and a
sticker does not carry the floor it was photographed on.

The fill runs at the master's full 7680px and the result is scaled down
afterwards. That ordering is what makes the edges soft — filling at the output
size gives a hard binary alpha and a jagged silhouette, while downsampling a
full-resolution mask averages it into a proper anti-aliased one.

**The pocket pass was written, measured and deleted, and that is the part worth
remembering.** A handful of background regions are trapped between heads where
the border fill cannot reach them, exactly as this file predicted. Every
tolerance loose enough to find them also took drool drips, the puddles under
them, and a highlight out of one eye — because a pale drip sealed by outlines
is indistinguishable from a trapped pocket by colour, by shape and by size.
Rendering the removed pixels in red over the artwork is what showed it; the
counts alone looked reasonable. The pockets that survive are the paper's own
colour, so they are invisible on a light page and read as part of the sticker
edge on a dark one. That is a far cheaper defect than an animal that has
stopped drooling.

The hue test this file used to recommend — neutral backdrop against blue, green
and yellow drool — was not needed in the end, because the plateau is wide enough
that colour never has to be consulted. Keep it in mind if a future redraw puts
the paper closer to the drool.

### After a redraw

The artwork version is named in two places: `site/hugo.toml`, which mounts the
cut-out and the backdrop, and `gen-site-images.go`, which cuts the icons from
the cut-out. Change both, run the two generators, and look at the result —
`-verify` guards the recovered constants, not the composition.
